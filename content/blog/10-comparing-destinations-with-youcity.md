You have narrowed the trip down to three destinations. Each has appealing photographs, interesting places to visit and someone who insists it is the best choice.

Another ranking may not help. What you need is a way to compare the trips you would actually take.

Use the same questions for each destination, then combine your impressions with practical checks. YouCity can help with the discovery part of that process.

## Give the comparison a clear brief

Write one sentence describing the trip: “Three nights away, two full days to explore, with one main activity and time to wander.”

Add your real constraints, such as dates, departure airport, budget or access requirements. A destination can be appealing and still be a poor fit for this particular journey.

Keep the shortlist small enough that you can investigate each option properly.

## Ask the same questions in each city

Explore the available videos and make three notes for every destination:

1. What caught my attention?
2. What would I like to do there?
3. What do I still need to verify?

Avoid giving one destination a detailed research session and another only a quick glance. Where comparable video modes are available, use them as a starting point.

Still account for differences in weather, routes and recording dates. A polished aerial edit and an overcast street walk create different impressions; that is not a fair measure of the destinations themselves.

## Separate observations from assumptions

“I enjoyed the view in this recording” is an observation about your response. “I would enjoy staying in this neighbourhood” is a possibility to investigate. “This area is always quiet” requires evidence a short video cannot provide.

Keep these distinctions in your notes. They make it easier to know what needs checking before you spend money.

If you add radio, consider it a separate discovery. Enjoying a station may add interest to the session, but it does not establish what the streets will sound like during your visit.

## Compare the trip as well as the destination

| Decision | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Time available | Arrival, departure and transfer arrangements |
| Total cost | Matching dates, accommodation standards and booking conditions |
| Main activity | Availability and any reservations needed |
| Getting around | Actual journeys from possible accommodation |
| Personal fit | Whether the planned activities genuinely interest you |

Use current information for these checks. When an important detail is unresolved, write “unknown” rather than awarding the city an optimistic score.

## Choose the trade-off you prefer

One option may offer more convenient travel; another may have the activity you most want. Decide which matters more for this trip.

You do not have to prove that the city you choose is universally better. You need a trip whose compromises you understand and whose plans make you want to go.

## Begin your shortlist

You can start with [São Paulo on YouCity](/city/sao-paulo), then choose another destination from the city browser. Use the same observation prompts for both and keep practical verification separate.

Finish with a decision—or one specific unanswered question. Either is more useful than collecting another dozen places without getting closer to a trip.
