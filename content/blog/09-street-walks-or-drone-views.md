From above, you may notice the shape of a waterfront. At street level, your attention might go to a doorway, a crossing or somewhere to sit.

Both perspectives can spark a travel idea. Choosing where to start is easier when you know what you are trying to discover.

## Start with a street walk for everyday details

A walking video places the camera closer to the surroundings you would encounter on foot. Depending on the route and framing, it may show shopfronts, surfaces, entrances and transitions between streets.

This can be useful when your question is personal: would I enjoy spending time exploring somewhere like this?

It is less useful for drawing conclusions about the entire city. One route leaves out other districts, and the recording does not establish present-day access or conditions.

## Start with an aerial view for broader context

An aerial recording can help you notice relationships between larger features: buildings, open spaces, water and the wider landscape.

Use it to discover something you want to locate or understand. Then consult a map for actual distances and connections.

An apparently short distance from above may not correspond to a direct pedestrian route. Aerial footage cannot confirm entrances, crossings or whether a path is open to the public.

## Match the view to the question

| Your question | Useful starting point | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What surroundings appeal to me? | Street walk | Identify places through route information |
| How are larger features arranged? | Aerial view | Check a map |
| Could I walk between two places? | Map and street-level context | Verify the actual walking route |
| Is access suitable for my needs? | Current detailed access information | Ask the relevant operator if unclear |

Neither perspective replaces current practical information. Choose a source according to the decision you need to make.

## Combine them without assuming they match

If both modes are available, begin with one and use the other to answer a follow-up question. You might spot an area from above and then look for street-level footage of it.

Check the creator's location details first. Two videos labelled with the same city may cover entirely different areas, seasons and dates. They should not be presented as matching views of a route unless that has been verified.

## Try a perspective change

Open [London on YouCity](/city/london) and choose from the modes currently available. Begin with the perspective that interests you, then switch if you have another question to explore.

Finish by saving one place to research. The point is to find a useful next step, not to watch every available video before deciding where to travel.
