Your first Paris itinerary can fill up surprisingly quickly. The Eiffel Tower, the Louvre, Montmartre, a river cruise, another museum—and suddenly every hour belongs to a reservation.

Leave a little space.

Three days give you time for a first taste of Paris: its famous landmarks, a few neighbourhoods and the pleasure of stopping somewhere simply because you like the view.

This itinerary groups each day around a different part of the city. Choose one main attraction, leave room for breaks, and use public transport whenever another walk feels like work.

Before you go, you can also [explore Paris on YouCity](/city/paris). Watch the available city videos and start noticing what catches your attention.

## Your three days at a glance

| Day | Main focus                     | Leave time for                                     |
| --- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Eiffel Tower and the Seine     | Riverside views and an unhurried lunch             |
| 2   | Louvre and the historic centre | Tuileries Garden and Île de la Cité                |
| 3   | Montmartre                     | Side streets, a café stop and a flexible afternoon |

Treat these as three interchangeable days. Move your Louvre visit if it falls on a Tuesday, when the museum is closed. Check your dates against its [official opening calendar](https://www.louvre.fr/en/visit/hours-admission).

## Day 1: The Eiffel Tower and the Seine

### Start with the view

Begin around Trocadéro for a view towards the Eiffel Tower, then make your way towards the river.

Give yourself time to look around before moving on. On a first visit, it is easy to spend the whole morning navigating, checking tickets and taking photographs without really pausing.

If going up the tower matters to you, make that your main booked activity for the day. Choose the level and access option that suit your interests and mobility, then arrange the rest of the day around your ticket.

The tower recommends booking ahead, especially for summit visits. Consult the [official Eiffel Tower planning guide](https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/planning-smooth-visit) for ticket options and practical information.

### Keep the afternoon open

After your visit, take a proper lunch break. Then choose between spending more time near the tower or following part of the Seine.

You do not need to walk all the way to another major attraction. Pick a stretch of river, enjoy it, and turn back or take transport when you have had enough.

A river cruise is another option if you would like to keep sightseeing while resting your feet. Compare the departure point, duration and what is included before booking.

### Let the evening depend on your energy

For your first evening, keep dinner convenient to where you finish—or where you are staying.

An itinerary that requires crossing the city again after a long day may look exciting on paper. A nearby table and time to talk about what you saw can be more appealing in practice.

## Day 2: The Louvre and the historic centre

### Choose what you want from the Louvre

Before booking, ask yourself a simple question: do you want a focused introduction or a substantial museum day?

Both are reasonable choices. What rarely works well is expecting to see everything and still keep a packed afternoon schedule.

Select a few collections or artworks that interest you. Allow space for getting between rooms and discovering something you did not come looking for.

The Louvre recommends time-slot reservations, including for visitors eligible for free admission. Check its [official admission information](https://www.louvre.fr/en/visit/hours-admission) before arranging your visit.

### Take a break in the Tuileries

After the museum, head towards the Tuileries Garden if you want some time outdoors.

This is a good point to reassess the day. If the Louvre has taken most of your energy, shorten the afternoon. You have already done the day's main activity.

If museums are not your priority, you can instead enjoy the Louvre’s exterior and devote more time to the gardens and surrounding streets.

### Continue towards Île de la Cité

For the afternoon, make your way towards Île de la Cité and the surroundings of Notre-Dame. Walk if you feel comfortable with the distance; otherwise, use transport for part of the journey.

Spend some time around the river and bridges rather than adding several more timed visits. If entering a monument is important to you, check its official visitor information separately before the trip.

Continue into the Latin Quarter if you still feel like exploring, or find somewhere to sit and end the sightseeing there.

The aim is to enjoy this part of Paris without turning the afternoon into another checklist.

## Day 3: Montmartre and your own version of Paris

### Give Montmartre a morning

Make Montmartre the focus of your final morning, with Sacré-Cœur as a landmark to orient yourself.

Allow for slopes and steps when planning your route. If you have mobility requirements, check suitable access options in advance rather than assuming that the shortest walking route will work for you.

Once you have seen the main sights, leave time for a slower wander. Choose a side street, browse a shop or stop for a coffee.

You do not need a new attraction every few minutes to make the morning worthwhile.

### Leave your last afternoon undecided

By now, you will have a better idea of what you enjoy in Paris.

Use the afternoon for that:

* Return to a neighbourhood you wanted to explore further.
* Add another museum if art has been the highlight.
* Spend more time by the river.
* Have a long lunch and browse without a schedule.

If you are considering Versailles, treat it as a substantial change to this itinerary. Replace a large part of a day rather than squeezing it into an already full afternoon.

## Preview Paris before you book

Photographs show individual scenes. A city video can help you observe the spaces between them: streets, crossings, buildings and the rhythm of a recorded journey.

Open [Paris on YouCity](/city/paris) and watch the available footage with three questions in mind:

1. What kinds of surroundings make me want to stop and explore?
2. Do I feel more drawn to landmarks, riverside views or everyday streets?
3. Would I enjoy a walking-heavy trip, or prefer shorter outings with more breaks?

Where a local radio station is available, try listening alongside the video for another way to discover the city’s atmosphere.

Treat this as inspiration. Recorded footage does not confirm current crowds, weather, accessibility or business opening hours, and it may not cover the stops in this itinerary.

## Before you finalise your plans

Book the experiences you care about most first, then build a flexible day around each one.

Check official opening information for your dates. Look at travel distances from your accommodation. Leave enough time for meals, transport and the occasional wrong turn.

Most of all, leave room to change your mind.

Your favourite moment in Paris might be the view you reserved months ahead. It might also be the street you decided to follow after lunch.
